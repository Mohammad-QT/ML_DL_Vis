code: |
  import numpy as np
  url = "https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Mohammad-QT/ML_DL_Vis/main/datasets/ad_clicks.csv"
  clicks = np.loadtxt(url, delimiter=",", skiprows=1, dtype=int)
  plays = np.zeros(clicks.shape[1], dtype=int)
  wins = np.zeros_like(plays)
  rng = np.random.default_rng(2026)
  for t in range(len(clicks)):
      samples = rng.beta(1 + wins, 1 + plays - wins)
      ad = np.argmax(samples)
      reward = clicks[t, ad]
      plays[ad] += 1
      wins[ad] += reward
  print(wins.sum())
---
Imagine trying a new ad. After a single click, its true rate could still be low or high. Instead of giving the ad one fixed score, Thompson sampling keeps a <mark>distribution</mark>: a curve that describes which click rates are plausible given the results so far.

Each round, sample a possible rate from every curve and show the ad with the highest draw. An ad you have barely tried can draw a high rate and get another chance. As you collect evidence, good ads usually draw high rates more often. Like UCB, this policy only learns from the ad it shows.
