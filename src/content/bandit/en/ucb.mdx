code: |
  import numpy as np
  url = "https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Mohammad-QT/ML_DL_Vis/main/datasets/ad_clicks.csv"
  clicks = np.loadtxt(url, delimiter=",", skiprows=1, dtype=int)
  plays = np.zeros(clicks.shape[1], dtype=int)
  wins = np.zeros_like(plays)
  for t in range(len(clicks)):
      if t < len(plays):
          ad = t
      else:
          scores = wins / plays + np.sqrt(2 * np.log(t) / plays)
          ad = np.argmax(scores)
      reward = clicks[t, ad]
      plays[ad] += 1
      wins[ad] += reward
  print(wins.sum())
---
You have five ads and room to show one each round. Each ad has a hidden chance of getting a click. A click counts as 1 and no click counts as 0. You only learn what happens for the ad you show. This is a <mark>multi-armed bandit</mark>: think of five slot machines, but you only get to pull one lever per round.

Do you keep showing the ad that looks best so far, or try an ad you know less about? The first is <mark>exploitation</mark>; the second is <mark>exploration</mark>. UCB balances them by giving less-tested ads a bigger bonus. A click still depends on chance: even the best ad can miss, and the policy that knows the best ad is a benchmark, not a guarantee of the most clicks in every run.
