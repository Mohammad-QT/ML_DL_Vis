Say you flag emails as spam. The <mark>threshold</mark> is the smallest score that counts as spam. Lower the threshold and you may catch more spam, but you may also flag more real mail.

**Precision** asks: of the emails you flagged, how many really were spam? **Recall** asks: of all the spam, how much did you catch? **F1** combines those two numbers and stays low if either one is low. Here we show zero when a ratio has nothing to divide by.

The **ROC curve** shows this trade-off across all thresholds. It plots recall against the fraction of real mail wrongly flagged. **AUC** is the area under that curve. The diagonal has area 0.5; a perfect ranking has area 1. Changing the cutoff moves the dot, not the curve, because the model’s scores stay the same. When positives are rare, also look at the precision–recall curve in the notebook: it shows how many of your alerts are right at each recall level.
