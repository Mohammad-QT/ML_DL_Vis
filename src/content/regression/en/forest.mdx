One tree is easy to read but jumpy: change a few points and its steps move. A <mark>random forest</mark> grows many trees, each on a slightly different random sample of the data, and takes the <b>average</b>. The jumpiness cancels out and the curve gets smoother and more reliable.

It is one of the strongest models out of the box: no scaling, few settings, and rarely a bad overfit.
